# Controversion Evidence System

## Purpose

Controversion should evaluate evidence without deciding what users must believe.

A source's credibility and the strength of a piece of evidence are separate measurements.

---

## 1. Source Types

Sources may include:

- Primary documents
- Scientific research
- Government records
- Court records
- Public datasets
- Photographs, video, and audio
- Expert testimony
- Witness testimony
- Investigative journalism
- News reporting
- Books
- Websites
- Social media
- Anonymous sources

Source type alone does not determine whether information is reliable.

---

## 2. Source Credibility

Evaluate sources using several factors.

### Proximity

How close is the source to the original event or information?

### Expertise

Does the source have relevant knowledge or qualifications?

### Transparency

Can users determine where the information came from?

### Corroboration

Do independent sources support the same information?

### Track Record

Does the source have a history of accurate reporting or documented corrections?

### Conflicts of Interest

Could financial, political, personal, or organizational interests influence the source?

---

## 3. Evidence Strength

Evidence should be evaluated independently from source credibility.

Possible ratings:

- Very Weak
- Weak
- Moderate
- Strong
- Very Strong

Factors may include:

- Directness
- Verifiability
- Independent corroboration
- Authenticity
- Reproducibility
- Completeness
- Alternative interpretations

---

## 4. Evidence Direction

Each piece of evidence can be classified as:

- Supports
- Challenges
- Neutral
- Unclear

This describes its relationship to a claim, not whether the evidence itself is trustworthy.

---

## 5. Confidence

Controversion should avoid reducing complex controversies to simple TRUE/FALSE labels.

Possible confidence levels:

1. Very Unlikely
2. Unlikely
3. Uncertain
4. Plausible
5. Likely
6. Very Likely

Every confidence assessment should include an explanation.

---

## 6. Disagreement

Users should be able to see when credible sources disagree.

Disagreement should not automatically be treated as evidence that both positions are equally strong.

The platform should show:

- What the sources agree on
- What they disagree on
- Evidence supporting each position
- Important missing information

---

## 7. Community Ratings

Users may eventually rate:

- How convincing evidence is
- Source credibility
- Their confidence in a claim

Community ratings must remain separate from evidence-based assessments.

Popularity does not determine truth.

---

## 8. Transparency

Users should be able to understand why an assessment exists.

Whenever practical, Controversion should expose:

Source → Evidence → Assessment → Reasoning

Assessments should be revisable when new evidence appears.

---

## Core Rule

Controversion evaluates evidence.

It does not decide what users are allowed to believe.
