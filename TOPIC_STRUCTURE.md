# Controversion Topic Structure

Every topic in Controversion should follow a consistent structure.

## 1. Topic

**Title:**  
Short, neutral description of the controversy.

**Summary:**  
Brief explanation of what is disputed and why the subject is controversial.

**Category:**  
Examples: Science, Politics, History, Technology, Health, Paranormal, Religion, Current Events.

**Tags:**  
Keywords used for search and related-topic discovery.

**Status:**  
Active, Historical, Developing, or Resolved.

---

## 2. Central Claim

Clearly state the main claim being examined.

The claim should be presented without assuming that it is true or false.

---

## 3. Supporting Evidence

For each piece of supporting evidence record:

- Evidence title
- Description
- Source
- Source type
- Publication date
- Direct link
- Reliability assessment
- Why it supports the claim

---

## 4. Counterevidence

Record evidence that challenges or contradicts the central claim.

Use the same information fields as supporting evidence.

---

## 5. Alternative Explanations

List competing explanations for the available evidence.

Each explanation should describe:

- What it proposes
- Evidence supporting it
- Evidence against it
- Important unanswered questions

---

## 6. Sources

Sources should preferably trace information back to original material.

Examples:

- Government records
- Scientific papers
- Court documents
- Historical records
- Interviews
- Photographs/video
- News reporting
- Books
- Archives
- Public datasets

---

## 7. Credibility

Source credibility should be evaluated separately from whether the source supports a particular conclusion.

Possible factors:

- Primary vs. secondary source
- Expertise
- Independent corroboration
- Transparency
- Evidence provided
- Corrections history
- Conflicts of interest

---

## 8. Confidence

Instead of simply labeling something TRUE or FALSE, Controversion can represent degrees of confidence.

Example:

- Very unlikely
- Unlikely
- Uncertain
- Plausible
- Likely
- Very likely

The reasoning behind a confidence assessment should always be visible.

---

## 9. Open Questions

List important questions that remain unanswered.

These can guide future research and community investigation.

---

## 10. Change History

Major changes to a topic should be recorded.

Examples:

- New evidence discovered
- Source corrected
- Claim modified
- Confidence assessment changed
- Alternative explanation added

This allows users to see how the understanding of a controversy evolves over time.
